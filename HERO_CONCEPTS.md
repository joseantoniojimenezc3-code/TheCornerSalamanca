# The Corner Salamanca — Hero Concept Study

All five directions use the approved `dsc-5720` photograph and retain one action only: **View Menu**. They differ in how the image, brand and viewport behave. No concept introduces unapproved imagery.

## Concept 01 — The Quiet Cover

**Composition:** A cream page surrounds a portrait image like the cover of an independent magazine. The photograph occupies roughly 78% of the mobile viewport and 62% of desktop width. The brand sits outside the image.

**Photography placement:** Portrait on mobile with almost no crop. On desktop, the image is right aligned and remains tall rather than becoming a landscape crop.

**Typography hierarchy:** “The Corner” is a large serif masthead above the image; “Salamanca” is a smaller tracked line. The descriptor is a restrained sans-serif caption.

**Whitespace strategy:** Wide cream margins create object-like presence around the photograph. The empty left desktop column carries all typography.

**CTA placement:** Beneath the descriptor in the left column, visually separated from the image.

**Scroll behavior:** The whole composition leaves the viewport normally. A small progress line at the bottom of the text column suggests continuation.

**Assessment:** Refined and fashion-led, but less immersive than the hospitality brief requires. It makes the photograph feel observed rather than entered.

## Concept 02 — The Cinematic Field

**Composition:** A conventional full-viewport image with centered white wordmark, descriptor and button. A dark neutral overlay protects contrast.

**Photography placement:** Full bleed with a central crop. The plate remains centered while peripheral cake and drinks become secondary texture.

**Typography hierarchy:** Large centered wordmark, small uppercase descriptor, compact inverse CTA.

**Whitespace strategy:** Whitespace exists inside the centered content stack, but the image fills every other area.

**CTA placement:** Centered immediately below the descriptor.

**Scroll behavior:** The hero holds for one viewport; the scroll indicator sits at bottom center and fades as the user moves down.

**Assessment:** Strong legibility and immediate impact, but the centered overlay is familiar in premium hospitality and risks feeling like a polished template.

## Concept 03 — The Salon Split

**Composition:** A 5/7 desktop split: warm-black text on cream at left and edge-to-edge photography at right. Mobile reverses the emphasis with image first and text below.

**Photography placement:** The image preserves its portrait character. Desktop shows the active plate and hands; mobile presents nearly the complete frame.

**Typography hierarchy:** Oversized two-line serif title, small descriptor, one body sentence and CTA.

**Whitespace strategy:** The left field uses generous internal margins and a deliberately low content density.

**CTA placement:** Bottom of the text field, separated from the title by a large vertical interval.

**Scroll behavior:** On desktop, the text remains visually still while the image completes the first screen; mobile scrolls as a continuous editorial page.

**Assessment:** Very COS-like and easy to art-direct, but it divides desire from identity instead of fusing them.

## Concept 04 — The Living Masthead — Selected

**Composition:** The approved photograph fills the usable viewport. Warm-black brand typography occupies the photograph’s natural pale-marble space on the left, while the plate, hands and drinks remain the living subject to the right. Content is left aligned rather than conventionally centered.

**Photography placement:** Full bleed. Mobile uses a portrait crop centered slightly right so the main plate and action remain present while the left marble edge carries type. Desktop uses a center-right focal point and a shallow tonal wash only behind the text-safe region—not across the entire image.

**Typography hierarchy:** “The Corner” is the dominant serif display line; “Salamanca” is smaller and widely tracked below it. “Coffee · Brunch · Drinks” is a quiet sans-serif label. The header wordmark stays restrained, making the hero masthead the emotional statement.

**Whitespace strategy:** Whitespace is photographic rather than added as a box. The pale table surface becomes the layout field. Content occupies a narrow measure and leaves the lower half largely untouched so the food retains appetite and scale.

**CTA placement:** Directly below the descriptor in the left content column. Its warm-black fill feels native against the marble rather than floating over a darkened image.

**Scroll behavior:** A small vertical “Scroll” marker sits at the lower-left edge of the content container. It fades and translates slightly once the user leaves the first screen. The hero itself does not pin or parallax; the photograph moves naturally to protect performance and motion accessibility.

**Assessment:** This is the strongest direction. It makes full use of the actual photograph instead of forcing it into a generic overlay pattern. Brightness distinguishes the brand from predictable dark luxury, the asymmetry feels editorial, and the composition works with the existing mobile-first portrait source. It creates appetite, identity and calm in one frame.

## Concept 05 — The Reveal

**Composition:** The first viewport is almost entirely photography with only a small wordmark. The title, descriptor and CTA appear in a cream panel immediately below the fold.

**Photography placement:** Full-width portrait image, uncropped on mobile and broadly cropped on desktop.

**Typography hierarchy:** Minimal brand identifier over the image; oversized serif statement appears only after the initial scroll.

**Whitespace strategy:** Maximum visual quiet in the first screen, followed by a large cream typography field.

**CTA placement:** Below the fold inside the cream panel.

**Scroll behavior:** A pronounced scroll cue invites the user to reveal the proposition; no automatic transition or pinned effect.

**Assessment:** Artistically confident, but it delays the only conversion action and asks first-time visitors to work too hard to understand the offer.

## Final direction

**Concept 04 — The Living Masthead** will be implemented.

It succeeds because it is derived from the photograph’s real composition: pale negative space, a concentrated food story and visible human action. It avoids the two most common restaurant patterns—dark centered overlays and boxed split screens—while retaining immediate clarity, a single CTA and a full-width emotional image. The natural scroll keeps it fast, calm and accessible.

## Art-direction revision

Following visual review, Concept 04 proved too dependent on text placed over photography: technically resolved, but not sufficiently authored. The production hero has therefore evolved into a stronger hybrid of Concept 01 and Concept 03.

The revised direction uses a dedicated cream masthead field beside a tightly cropped image plane on desktop, and a deliberate text/image sequence within one viewport on mobile. Identity, CTA and negative space are now controlled independently from the photograph. This produces a clearer first hierarchy, stronger logo visibility and a more recognizably editorial composition while preserving the approved image and single-action brief.
